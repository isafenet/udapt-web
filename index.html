Features

Everything for your routine. Nothing you don’t need.

Udapt is built around how a plan actually needs to change day to day: how you slept, how the last session went, and what you've eaten. Every adjustment comes with a reason, never just a new number.

A readiness score you can understand

A score out of 10 each morning, built from your own Apple Health data — how your heart and sleep compare with your normal — plus a 5-question check-in. No Watch at night? The check-in alone works. Tap Why? for charts and the research behind it.

Workouts that ease off when you need it

After a rough night, Udapt drops a few sets and asks you to stop each set with a few reps to spare. On good days your weights go up. Prefer the original workout? It’s always one tap away.

Food targets that move with training

More carbs on training days, a little less on rest days — the week's total stays the same. After two weeks of logging, your calorie target adapts to what your body actually does.

Log food your way

Search thousands of foods, scan a barcode, or snap a photo or say what you ate — Apple Intelligence works it out on your phone, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Protect a joint

Flag a shoulder, knee or lower back you're being careful with, and Udapt automatically swaps exercises that load it for a like-for-like alternative that doesn't.

Home or gym

Full programs for a commercial gym, or a dumbbells-and-bodyweight-only variant for training at home. Switch any time.

Ask Siri

“How ready am I?” or “Log my usual breakfast” — hands-free, from your phone or your wrist.

Private by design

No ads, no third-party analytics. Health data stays in Apple Health; anything Udapt-only stays on your phone.

Backed by research

Every rule has a reason. Every reason has a source.

Udapt’s rules are checked against published studies, read at the source. Where the evidence is thin, or something is simply our choice, we say so.

How you feel counts

A review of 56 studies found that how people say they feel picks up training strain at least as well as heart or blood tests. So your morning check-in counts as much as your heart data.

Saw et al., 2016 · British Journal of Sports Medicine

One bad night won’t ruin your week

Heart rate variability jumps around night to night, so Udapt uses your 7-night average — the approach research found most reliable.

Plews et al., 2013 · Sports Medicine

Seven hours of sleep matters

Sleep experts agree adults need at least 7 hours. Less than that counts against your score, even if it’s normal for you.

Watson et al., 2015 · Sleep

Protein that never drops

About 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight is where most of the muscle-building benefit is reached. Udapt starts you there and never cuts it on easy days.

Morton et al., 2018 · British Journal of Sports Medicine

More food on training days

Training days get a little more, rest days a little less, and the week adds up the same. After two weeks, your target adapts to how your weight actually moves.

Hall & Chow, 2011 · American Journal of Clinical Nutrition

Honest about the limits

Adjusting training day by day helps you manage tiredness — studies found it doesn’t build extra muscle on its own. We’d rather tell you than oversell it.

Bittencourt et al., 2024 · Frontiers in Physiology

FAQ

Before you ask.

How is Udapt different from other fitness apps?

Most apps adjust your training or your food, not both together. Udapt reads Apple Health each morning and adjusts today's training and today's food targets as one connected plan, and explains every change in plain English.

Do I need an Apple Watch?

No. A 5-question morning check-in is enough to adapt your plan if you don't wear a Watch overnight. If you do have Apple Health data, Udapt uses that too.

Is Udapt's advice evidence-based?

Yes. Every rule is checked against published studies, and each one is labelled by how strong the evidence is — from “strong evidence” to “our design choice”. Tap Why? in the app to see the sources, or read The science.

What does the readiness score mean?

It’s a score out of 10 showing how recovered you look compared with your own normal, built from your Apple Health data (heart rate variability, resting heart rate and sleep) and your morning check-in. 7 means you’re at your normal. Go (7–10) means train as planned, Ease off (4–6) means a lighter session, and Recover (0–3) suggests a walk or rest. How it works.

Does Udapt have an Apple Watch app?

It’s on the way, with set logging using the Digital Crown, a rest timer, and your readiness score on your watch face. You don’t need it: your Watch already feeds Udapt through Apple Health.

Is my health data private?

Udapt is built on-device with no servers. Your data never leaves your phone, except a barcode number when you choose to scan one.

Does it work for home workouts?

Yes — a dumbbells-and-bodyweight-only program variant is built in alongside the full-gym programs, and you can switch any time.

What if I'm training around an injury?

Flag a joint you're being careful with in Settings, and Udapt swaps out exercises that load it for a like-for-like alternative where one exists. This isn't medical advice — always check with a physiotherapist or doctor about what's safe for you.
