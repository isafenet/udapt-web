One morning. One plan that fits the day.

Your strength sessions, your runs, rides or swims and your food targets, planned together and checked against how you've recovered.

Before you ask

Questions, answered.

How is Udapt different from other fitness apps?

Udapt plans your strength training, your runs, rides or swims and your food targets as one plan, checks each morning how you've recovered, and explains every change in plain English, with the research behind it.

Do I need an Apple Watch?

No. A four-question morning check-in gives you a readiness on its own, and runs and rides work on iPhone. With an Apple Watch, Udapt also uses your heart signals overnight, records swims in the water, and gives you the Watch app.

Is Udapt's advice evidence-based?

Yes. Every rule comes from published research or official guidance, and each is labelled by how directly it applies: Direct, Applied, or Udapt's choice where no study sets a number. The Why? buttons in the app and the Science page show the sources.

What does my readiness mean?

Go, Ease off or Recover, from two checks: your heart signals compared with your own last 10 readings, and your morning check-in. Each check outside your usual range moves the day down a step. There's no score out of 10, because no study gives one.

Does Udapt plan running, cycling and swimming?

Yes: a nine-week run/walk plan, running towards a goal or a race, easy rides, and pool or open-water swims, at your level and on the days you choose, fitted around your strength training.

Do other apps and devices count?

Yes. Runs, rides and swims that other apps and devices save to Apple Health count in your plan, alongside Udapt's own.

Does Udapt have an Apple Watch app?

Yes, included with your subscription: the morning check-in, your readiness and plan, set logging with heart rate and a rest timer, runs, rides and swims, and complications for readiness, today's session and protein.

Is my health data private?

Udapt is built on-device. Your health data, profile, workouts and food logs stay on your iPhone (and your own Apple Watch, if you use the Watch app), with one disclosed exception: scanning a barcode sends only the barcode number to Open Food Facts, a nonprofit food database.

Does it work for home workouts?

Yes. Tell Udapt what you have, from a pair of dumbbells to a multi-gym, or train with resistance bands, in a hotel gym or on the floor. You choose where for each session.

What if I'm training around an injury?

Flag a joint you're being careful with in Settings, and Udapt swaps out exercises that load it for a like-for-like alternative where one exists. It's not medical advice: always check with a doctor or health professional about what's safe for you. If your health or rehab team has given you an exercise plan, add it in My own plan: Udapt logs it exactly as it's written and never changes it.

What languages does Udapt speak?

English, Spanish, French, German, Italian and Portuguese (Portugal and Brazil), including every exercise how-to. It follows your iPhone's language. Food search still uses English food names for now.
