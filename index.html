New in Udapt 1.2

For the days that don't go to plan.

Feeling rough, away from home, on a GLP-1 medicine, or just worn down: Udapt 1.2 changes the day to fit, and tells you why. It's a free update for everyone.

GLP-1 mode

One switch in Settings. Tap Queasy today or Not hungry today in the morning check-in and the session gets gentler, while the food card puts protein first. Udapt never asks about your medicine or dose.

Unwell days

Tell Udapt it's a cold, a fever or aching all over and today becomes a rest day with your food target held steady. When you're better, the first week back is lighter.

Travelling

Sessions swap to exercises you can do in a hotel room, at a lighter week's volume. Udapt offers this when your iPhone changes time zone.

A real lighter week

After a third rough day in a row, Udapt offers a lighter week: fewer sets and easier effort for seven days, then back to normal.

Most apps plan training and food separately. Udapt treats them as one plan, adjusted daily from Apple Health.

Before you ask

Questions, answered.

How is Udapt different from other fitness apps?

Most apps adjust your training or your food, not both together. Udapt reads Apple Health each morning and adjusts today's training and today's food targets as one connected plan, and explains every change in plain English rather than leaving you guessing.

Do I need an Apple Watch?

No. Udapt works from a 5-question morning check-in if you don't wear a Watch overnight, and still adapts your plan from that alone. If you do have Apple Health data, it uses that too.

Is Udapt's advice evidence-based?

Yes. Every rule is checked against published studies, and each one is labelled by how strong the evidence is, from strong evidence to our own design choice. The Why? buttons in the app and the Science page on this website show the sources.

What does the readiness score mean?

A score out of 10 showing how recovered you look compared with your own normal, built from your Apple Health data (heart rate variability, resting heart rate and sleep) and your morning check-in. 7 means you are at your normal.

Does Udapt have an Apple Watch app?

Yes, it is included with your subscription. Log sets with the Digital Crown, get a rest timer with a haptic when it is done, and see your readiness score on your watch face. You do not need to use it: your Watch already feeds Udapt through Apple Health either way.

Is my health data private?

Udapt is built on-device. Your health data, profile, workouts and food logs stay on your iPhone (and your own Apple Watch, if you use the Watch app), with one disclosed exception: scanning a barcode sends only the barcode number to Open Food Facts, a nonprofit food database.

Does it work for home workouts?

Yes — a dumbbells-and-bodyweight-only program variant is built in alongside the full-gym programs, and you can switch any time.

What if I'm training around an injury?

Flag a joint you're being careful with in Settings, and Udapt swaps out exercises that load it for a like-for-like alternative where one exists. It's not medical advice — always check with a physiotherapist or doctor about what's safe for you. If a physio, doctor or rehab team has given you an exercise plan, add it in My own plan: Udapt logs it exactly as it's written and never changes it.

What languages does Udapt speak?

English, Spanish, French, German, Italian and Portuguese (Portugal and Brazil), including every exercise how-to. It follows your iPhone's language. Food search still uses English food names for now.
