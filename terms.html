Udapt is software. It is not a personal trainer, coach, physiotherapist, doctor, nurse or registered dietitian, and nobody at Udapt acts as one for you. It does not assess you in person, watch your technique or know your full medical history.

Udapt is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease or condition, and it is not for medical emergencies. If you think you need urgent medical help, call your local emergency number (999 in the UK).

Its training and food suggestions are general guidance based on published research and the information you give it. They are not a substitute for advice from a qualified professional who knows you.

4 Exercise safety and your choices

  • Check first. Talk to a doctor or physiotherapist before you start any exercise programme, change your diet significantly, or exercise with an injury, a health condition, a pregnancy or medicine that affects your heart rate or blood pressure.
  • Answer the health check honestly and update it in Settings if anything changes. Udapt uses it to step back from planning your training when that's safer.
  • Your choices are yours. Udapt may suggest a starting level, but you choose your level, your activities, your exercises and the weights you lift, and you can change them at any time. A suggestion is never an instruction, and you never have to do more than you're comfortable with.
  • Stop if it hurts. Stop straight away and get medical advice if you feel pain, chest pain or tightness, dizziness, faintness, unusual breathlessness or an irregular heartbeat.
  • Exercise has risks. All physical activity carries some risk of injury, and in rare cases serious harm, even when it is done carefully. Learn new exercises with a qualified instructor if you can, and use equipment that is safe and suitable.
  • Food targets are estimates. Calorie and nutrient figures are estimates, not a prescription. Speak to your doctor or a registered dietitian before following a calorie target if you have, or have had, an eating disorder, diabetes or another condition affected by diet.

This section explains the risks so you can make informed choices. It does not remove our responsibility where we have been negligent (see section 12).

5 Where exercises, plans and food data come from

  • Exercises: Udapt did not create the exercises it shows. Their photos and instructions come from free-exercise-db, a public-domain collection, and are credited in the app.
  • Plans: Udapt's rules follow published research and guidance, each listed with its source on The science page. The run/walk plan follows the structure of the NHS Couch to 5K plan; Udapt is not connected with or endorsed by the NHS or any other body whose guidance it cites.
  • Food data: the bundled food database draws on USDA FoodData Central, UK CoFID, ANSES-CIQUAL and the Australian Food Composition Database, each under its own open licence (see Settings › About). Barcode lookups use Open Food Facts. We can't guarantee the accuracy of data supplied by these third parties, so check labels where it matters to you.
